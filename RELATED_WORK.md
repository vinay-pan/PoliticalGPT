## Papers requiring individual differentiation (≥2 of 3 axes per D-11)

### The Amplifying Mirror (arXiv:2606.08792, June 2026)

**What it does:** Logistic-regression probes on Llama 3.1 8B Instruct hidden states across all 32 layers, trained on 190,491 Congressional tweets (Sept 2016–Feb 2023, R/D labeled). Identifies a single partisan direction at layer 18 (AUC 0.945, Cohen's d 1.94). Two causal interventions — **ablation** (project activations onto the hyperplane orthogonal to the partisan direction) and **amplification** (add α·ω̂, α_frac ∈ [0.15, 0.3], graded/continuous) — evaluated on 150 prompts spanning contested policy issues. Results: stance inversion, register shifting, and named-entity fabrication of plausible attributed quotes. No fine-tuning, LoRA, adapters, or counterfactual-prompt-pair generation anywhere in the paper — the entire mechanism is activation-space geometry on a single frozen chat model.

**Axes matched:** (a) continuous-steering mechanism — yes (graded α amplification); (b) partisan framing as target attribute — yes (direct); (c) counterfactual/controlled-stimulus design — partial (same prompt set across intervention strengths is structurally similar to holding a stimulus constant, but not a paired-generation design and doesn't invent novel events). **2-3 of 3 axes.**

**How we differ:** The Amplifying Mirror locates a single partisan-identity direction in Llama 3.1 8B Instruct's activation space via probing on Congressional tweets, and steers it with graded activation-space interventions on general policy-discourse prompts. This project differs on mechanism (LoRA adapter weight-space interpolation between two independently fine-tuned outlet adapters, not activation-space steering of one frozen model — no fine-tuning occurs in their pipeline at all), on target attribute (outlet journalistic house style — CNN vs. Fox institutional framing, encoded via supervised fine-tuning on real outlet corpora — vs. their Congressional-tweet-derived R/D party-identity axis), on output domain (headline+body news-article generation on invented events vs. their discursive policy-stance completions), and on measurement (three literature-grounded framing axes with CI-based aggregate reporting and a preregistered λ dose-response decomposition, vs. their qualitative pattern characterization of ~150 prompts). Their finding that a single geometric direction is "uneven" under ablation is a relevant risk signal for this project's own λ=0 midpoint interpretability question and is worth citing as a reason to measure, not assume, dial coherence.

### Linear Representations of Political Perspective (arXiv:2503.02080, Kim, Evans & Schein, ICLR 2025)

**What it does:** Probes attention-head activations across three 7B chat models (Llama-2-7b-chat, Mistral-7b-instruct, Vicuna-7b) using prompts generated from the stated perspective of individual U.S. lawmakers, regressing head activations against each lawmaker's DW-NOMINATE ideology score. Predictive heads concentrate in middle layers. The same probe, trained only on lawmaker data, transfers to predicting news outlets' ideological slant. Linear interventions on the identified heads steer generation output toward liberal/conservative. No mention found of LoRA, adapter-based fine-tuning, counterfactual/paired-event prompts, or news-article generation as an output format. *Confidence note: the PDF fetch failed during today's read (corrupted/binary stream) and the HTML variant returned only abstract-plus-metadata-level detail beyond the 2026-08-26 pass — treat this differentiation line as MEDIUM confidence pending an actual PDF-reader pass.*

**Axes matched:** (a) continuous-steering mechanism — yes (linear attention-head interventions, graded); (b) political ideology as target attribute — yes; (c) counterfactual/controlled-stimulus design — no (probing + transfer-learning to real outlet slant prediction is an observational/classification design). **2 of 3 axes.**

**How we differ:** Kim, Evans & Schein find that political ideology is linearly decodable from attention-head activations in three general-purpose 7B chat models, and show the same linear probe transfers to predicting real news outlets' ideological slant — evidence that "outlet identity" and "general political ideology" are entangled in these models' representations. This project differs on mechanism (adapter weight-space interpolation vs. activation-space linear probing/steering — no fine-tuning in their pipeline), on evaluation design (interpretability/probing-and-transfer, evaluated by probe-predicted stance shift, vs. this project's counterfactual paired-generation design scored on literature-grounded framing axes with human-calibrated agreement), and on scope (general political ideology across three base chat models vs. two specific news outlets' journalistic style, fine-tuned into two separate adapters on a single base). Their finding that a lawmaker-ideology probe transfers to outlet-slant prediction is directly relevant supporting evidence for this project's own base-model-priors/latent-ideology risk — it strengthens the case that this project's adapters could partly be re-expressing a general ideology axis the base model already encodes, rather than outlet-specific style.

## Single-axis overlaps (grouped summary)

**LLM-generated political-bias measurement work (target attribute only).** "Quantifying Generative Media Bias with a Corpus of Real-world and Generated News Articles" (arXiv:2406.10773) compares 2,100 human articles against 56,700 LLM-generated articles across 9 LLMs, finding instruction-tuned models show consistent political bias; prompts are derived from real article descriptions, not invented counterfactual events, and no fine-tuning/adapter mechanism is used (axes: (a) no, (b) yes, (c) partial — 1-1.5/3). LLM political-bias-auditing/benchmarking papers surfaced in the Feng et al. 2023 citation sweep (POW, PoliticsBench, Overton-window studies) share the same profile: measurement of an existing model's political bias via prompting, not counterfactual-generation-plus-adapter-interpolation work.

**Media-frame classification and generalization work (target attribute only, no steering or counterfactual design).** "Generalizability of Media Frames: Corpus creation and analysis across countries" (arXiv:2506.16337) extends the MFC's 15 frames to Brazilian Portuguese news via classifier zero-shot evaluation with 8 chat-instructed LLMs plus GPT-4o — a classification/annotation-generalization study, not a generation or steering pipeline (axes: (a) no, (b) yes, (c) no — 1/3). "Retain or Reframe? A Computational Framework for the Analysis of Framing in News Articles and Reader Comments" (arXiv:2507.04612) analyzes 3,000+ real news articles and 380,000 reader comments across two outlets and eleven topics, observationally, finding readers reframe content at least as often as they retain it (axes: (a) no, (b) partial — general framing across topics, not specifically outlet-conditioned partisan framing, (c) no — observational, not counterfactual — roughly 1/3). "Framing Migration News with LLMs: Structured CoT as a Support for Human Interpretation" (arXiv:2606.03761) uses structured chain-of-thought prompting to support human frame classification of real migration news texts, evaluated via human annotator agreement — a classification-assistance tool, not a generation or steering pipeline (axes: (a) no, (b) yes — migration framing is politically adjacent, (c) no — 1/3). None of these three combine adapter interpolation, counterfactual invented-event prompts, or a continuous steering mechanism.
